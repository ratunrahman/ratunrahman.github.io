Rethinking Structured Generation: Can Graph-Based Reasoning Resolve Ambiguity?

Natural language may support multiple valid interpretations, but structured generators are often trained toward a single reference. We develop an ambiguity-aware approach that reasons over candidate graphs to better compare semantically plausible outputs.

  • Multi-valid structured prediction
  • Candidate graph construction and semantic comparison
  • Evaluation on structured generation benchmarks

04 / ABOUT

Curiosity, with rigor.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Engineering at the University of Alabama in Huntsville. I’m interested in a simple but difficult question: how can learning systems make better decisions when the data, the problem, or even the correct answer is uncertain?

My research approaches this question from several directions—language and graph-based reasoning, personalized and probabilistic federated learning, and multimodal learning. I value careful experimental design, clear problem formulations, and reproducible results.

I’m exploring Research Scientist, Applied Scientist, and research-focused ML roles beginning in January 2027.
