PROJECT / ONGOING · MANUSCRIPT UNDER REVIEW

When Evidence Conflicts: Interference-Aware Aggregation for Faithful Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Investigating retrieval-augmented generation when the retrieved context is noisy, contradictory, or only partially supportive of a claim.

Ratun RahmanManuscript under review · not peer-reviewed publicationRetrieval-augmented generation / Faithfulness / Evidence aggregation
Contact about this research ↗
Research status: Under review in 2026. This page describes the research direction and proposed design, not an accepted publication or a released software package.

Problem

Standard retrieval-augmented generation often concatenates retrieved passages into a prompt or ranks documents individually. That approach may fail when sources disagree, when evidence is redundant, or when the most confidently written passage is not the most reliable.

Research direction

01Retrieve evidence

Collect passages and candidate claims across a noisy, heterogeneous retrieval set.

02Model agreement and conflict

Reason about how passages support, contradict, or qualify one another rather than treating them independently.

03Aggregate for faithfulness

Use evidence-interference signals to guide response selection or generation, with uncertainty-aware behavior when support is weak.

Evaluation focus

The research plan includes multi-hop question answering, fact verification, and long-form answer generation, comparing evidence-aware aggregation with conventional RAG and reranking-style baselines. Important measures include answer correctness, faithfulness to retrieved sources, and behavior under deliberately contradictory retrieval.

Current boundaries

No numerical performance table is included, because publicly verifiable final results were not available when this portfolio page was prepared. This avoids presenting preliminary findings as established performance.
